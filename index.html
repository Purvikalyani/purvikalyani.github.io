I am a final-year undergraduate researcher pursuing a B.Sc. (Hons) with Research in Psychology (concentration in Chemistry) at Krea University, working in the Computational Cognition Laboratory under the supervision of Dr. Rakesh Sengupta.

My research explores how the human brain dynamically organizes transient sensory inputs into structured internal representations across space and time. Undergraduate Capstone Thesis · May 2026 – Present

Unified OCOS Modeling & 64-Channel EEG Dynamics of Visual Working Memory Stability

Advisor: Dr. Rakesh Sengupta (Krea University) · Methods: Python, MNE-Python, 64-Channel EEG, Linear Mixed-Effects (LME)

Developing a unified recurrent On-Center Off-Surround (OCOS) network model integrating dynamic self-excitation and distance-dependent lateral inhibition to simulate visual working memory (VWM) stability boundaries and the von Restorff isolation effect. Empirical validation combines a 64-channel human EEG paradigm (N = 20; analyzing Contralateral Delay Activity [CDA], P3b, and beta-band oscillations via MNE-Python) with Linear Mixed-Effects (LME) model-to-brain fitting to quantitatively link computational parameters to neural markers.

Computational Theory · 2026

From Local Competition to Bump Attractors: Excitation Width, Capacity & Lattice Locking

Collaborator: Dr. Rakesh Sengupta · Methods: Dynamical Systems, Energy-Function Hessian Formalism

Placed discrete OCOS networks and continuous bump attractors into a single parameterized family governed by recurrent excitation width at fixed total excitation. Derived closed-form stability and capacity bounds for the narrow limit and demonstrated that widening excitation trades capacity and noise resistance for spatial continuity—while revealing that a subthreshold recruitment margin at item edges (ρ = 0.91) governs lattice locking versus memory capture by new inputs.

Recurrent Neural Modeling · 2024 – 2026

Time-Varying Self-Excitation & Serial Position Curves in Working Memory

Advisor: Dr. Rakesh Sengupta (Krea University) · Published in Springer CCIS (ANTIC 2025) & Presented at CogSci 2026 / ACCS 13

Methods & Analytical Framework: Simulated a fully connected N = 70 single-layer recurrent On-Center Off-Surround (OCOS) network over 10,000 ms using a piecewise time-varying self-excitation function α(t) that ramps linearly at slope κ during stimulus input and decays exponentially with time constant τ after offset. Derived exact homogeneous steady-state existence criteria (α − β(N − 1) > λ), global contraction bounds (α + β ≤ λ), and Jacobian/Lyapunov linear stability eigenmodes (λ1 uniform activity mode and λ2 transverse symmetry-breaking modes). Final activations were mapped to recall probabilities via a logistic readout (γ = 5, θ = 0.5) across 8-item serial recall sequences and four presentation durations (nstim ∈ {100, 200, 400, 800} time-steps; T = 50 independent trials per rate).

Simulation & Statistical Results: Modulating the post-input decay constant τ smoothly transitioned serial position curves from strongly recency-dominated (τ = 500 ms) to balanced/bimodal (τ = 1000–1250 ms) to primacy-dominated (τ = 1500 ms), while lateral inhibition β ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.15} governed the trade-off between coactive capacity and convergence speed. The model also replicated the classic Waugh & Norman presentation-rate effect, exhibiting a robust monotonic negative trend between per-item duration and the Primacy/Recency Index (Pearson r = −0.9395, p = 0.0605; 1,000-sample bootstrap 95% CI [−1.0000, −0.8615]; linear regression slope = −0.0024, bootstrap 95% CI [−0.0053, −0.0010]).

Behavioral Psychophysics · Jan 2025 – Mar 2025

Hearing Time vs. Seeing Time: Cross-Modal Temporal Discrimination Across Start and End Alignment

Advisor: Dr. Rakesh Sengupta (Krea University) · Presented at ACCS 13 (University of Hyderabad, Dec 2026)

Methods: Recruited N = 20 participants (aged 18–30) in a two-alternative forced-choice (2-AFC) psychophysical paradigm comparing paired visual (white square) and auditory (pure tone) stimuli across five durations (0.18, 0.27, 0.36, 0.45, and 0.54 s). Across 120 randomized trials per participant (ITI: 0.95–1.05 s), stimulus pairs were either start-aligned (simultaneous onset) or end-aligned (simultaneous offset), and participants judged which modality lasted longer.

Key Results: Across 2,450 trials (mean accuracy = 0.70, mean RT = 0.96 s), paired-samples t-tests showed significantly higher discrimination accuracy for end-aligned stimuli (M = 0.72) than start-aligned stimuli (M = 0.68; t = −2.26, p = 0.0242), peaking on end-aligned auditory-longer trials (0.756) versus start-aligned visual-longer trials (0.616). Accuracy scaled monotonically with absolute duration difference (psychometric slope β = 0.796, p < .001) and replicated auditory dominance (M = 0.75 when auditory > visual vs. M = 0.64 when visual > auditory; t = 2.66, p = 0.015), indicating that shared offsets avoid competing onset transients and visual sensory adaptation.

Manuscripts & Preprints

Prasannah, P., & Sengupta, R. (2026). From local competition to bump attractors: how excitation width sets capacity, interference and updating in recurrent models of visual working memory. arXiv:2611.28601 [q-bio.NC].

arXiv DOI ↗
On-centre off-surround (OCOS) networks and bump attractors, the two main recurrent models of visual working memory, differ chiefly in how far recurrent excitation spreads. Placing both in a unified family parameterized by excitation width at fixed total excitation, we solve the narrow OCOS limit analytically via the energy-function Hessian formalism and show that widening excitation trades capacity and noise resistance for spatial continuity—while revealing that items remain locked to the lattice via a subthreshold recruitment margin at their edges (ρ = 0.91), whose collapse causes new inputs to capture rather than join stored memories.
