Computational Cognitive Neuroscience & Visual Working Memory

I am a final-year undergraduate researcher pursuing a B.Sc. (Hons) with Research in Psychology (concentration in Chemistry) at Krea University, working in the Computational Cognition Laboratory under the supervision of Dr. Rakesh Sengupta.

My research explores how the human brain dynamically organizes transient sensory inputs into structured internal representations across space and time. To bridge algorithmic theories and their biological implementations, I combine recurrent neural network modeling (On-Center Off-Surround and bump-attractor dynamics), human electrophysiology (64-channel EEG-ERP and oscillatory analysis), and behavioral psychophysics.

Undergraduate Capstone Thesis · 2025–2026

Unified OCOS Modeling & 64-Channel EEG Dynamics of Visual Working Memory Stability

Advisor: Dr. Rakesh Sengupta · Methods: Python, MNE-Python, 64-Channel EEG, Linear Mixed-Effects (LME)

Developing a unified On-Center Off-Surround (OCOS) network model integrating dynamic self-excitation and distance-dependent lateral inhibition to account for visual working memory (VWM) stability boundaries and the von Restorff isolation effect. Empirically testing model predictions against 64-channel human EEG data (N = 20)—analyzing Contralateral Delay Activity (CDA), P3b, and beta-band oscillations via MNE-Python and LME model-to-brain fitting.

Computational Theory · 2025–2026

From Local Competition to Bump Attractors: Excitation Width, Capacity & Lattice Locking

Collaborator: Dr. Rakesh Sengupta · Methods: Dynamical Systems, Energy-Function Formalism, Numerical Continuation

Placed discrete OCOS networks and continuous bump attractors into a single parameterized family governed by recurrent excitation width at fixed total excitation. Derived closed-form stability and capacity bounds for the narrow limit and demonstrated that widening excitation trades capacity and noise resistance for spatial continuity—while revealing that a subthreshold recruitment margin at item edges (ρ = 0.91) governs lattice locking versus memory capture by new inputs.

Recurrent Neural Modeling · 2024–2025

Time-Varying Self-Excitation & Serial Position Curves in Working Memory

Advisor: Dr. Rakesh Sengupta · Methods: GNU Octave, Python, Dynamical Rate Networks

Simulated a recurrent OCOS architecture incorporating time-varying self-excitation across sequential item presentation. Showed that modulating the decay constant of self-excitation systematically reproduces classic primacy and recency serial position effects without requiring separate short- and long-term memory stores.

Behavioral Psychophysics · 2025–2026

Active Mental Manipulation vs. Passive Maintenance in Visual Working Memory

Methods: PsychoPy, Statistical Modeling in R (N = 30) · Presented at NAoP 2026

Designed a psychophysical paradigm contrasting passive serial recall against active mental sorting across varying set sizes and stimulus categories, demonstrating that active manipulation imposes significantly steeper load-dependent costs on recall accuracy and reaction time than maintenance alone.

Time Perception & Embodied Cognition · 2023–2024

Cross-Modal Temporal Perception & Embodied Timing in Percussionists

Methods: Psychophysics, Mixed Quantitative–Qualitative Analysis

Investigated temporal interval estimation across visual and auditory modalities, followed by a mixed-methods study examining how bodily, motor, and contextual constraints shape subjective time perception in trained percussionists.

Manuscripts & Preprints

Prasannah, P., & Sengupta, R. (2026). From local competition to bump attractors: how excitation width sets capacity, interference and updating in recurrent models of visual working memory. Preprint, Computational Cognition Laboratory, Krea University.

Preprint PDF (Coming Soon)
On-centre off-surround (OCOS) networks and bump attractors, the two main recurrent models of visual working memory, differ chiefly in how far recurrent excitation spreads. Placing both in a unified family parameterized by excitation width at fixed total excitation, we solve the narrow OCOS limit analytically via the energy-function Hessian formalism and show that widening excitation trades capacity and noise resistance for spatial continuity—while revealing that items remain locked to the lattice via a subthreshold recruitment margin at their edges (ρ = 0.91), whose collapse causes new inputs to capture rather than join stored memories.

Peer-Reviewed Conference Proceedings

Prasannah, P., & Sengupta, R. (2025). Modeling serial position effects in working memory using a recurrent on-center off-surround network with time-varying self-excitation. In Proceedings of the 5th International Conference on Advanced Network Technologies and Intelligent Computing (ANTIC).

We investigate the dynamical emergence of serial position curves in a competitive On-Center Off-Surround (OCOS) recurrent network augmented with time-varying self-excitation. Computational simulations demonstrate that varying the decay time constant of self-excitation accounts for both primacy and recency recall advantages within a single unified circuit mechanism.
